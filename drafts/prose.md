## AI (paragraph 1)

The damage generative AI did to the arts is more complicated than any one law or
overreach. A tool was made, and the people deciding either didn't consider its effects
or didn't care about them, and not considering them is not caring. At the start you could
get a minted copy of a working artist's style without paying them or ever speaking to
them. Whether that came from ignorance or malice doesn't concern me; the effect is the
same. Something went wrong. People were harmed in ways they didn't have to be, and I
haven't seen anyone try to ameliorate it.

I hold myself to the same standard. If someone tells me bypo doesn't account for their
accessibility, for a reason I never thought of, that's a fair thing to say, and how I
respond matters. I don't believe in a fixed right and wrong. I look at the situation and
do the best I can.

## How I work (bypo / general)

I wanted to explore design practically while learning DSP and development. Building bypo
taught me everything reading couldn't; some things reading just isn't enough for. I've
never read a book on music either. I learn by doing, usually while I'm doing it. I was
learning while I made almost every one of my albums. I don't set out with a picture of
the thing and go after it. I decide to build, and find out what I'm making, and why,
along the way.

Before I started, I'd been thinking about the nature of mathematics, physics and logic.
A digital synthesiser is mathematics simulating physics, so it felt honest to build the
instrument's architecture on that idea. Phase accumulators sit at the centre of
synthesis. The ideas inside them reach into so much else that understanding them made the
rest easier to understand. In systems that fundamental, anything unplanned is usually
unwanted.

## i miss the rain

*i miss the rain* was the first release under AKOL, but I'd been releasing music for
years before it. Most of that music doesn't exist any more, or it's buried in corners of
the internet I probably couldn't find again. Eventually I didn't want to be associated
with it, because I hadn't made it to be something I wanted to listen to. I'd made it to
meet some kind of standard. Once I'd improved, I could hear what I would have done
better, and it bothered me that those records stood for me online.

Nothing on *i miss the rain* tries to reach a standard. I explored, found, refined and
released. None of that gives me the discontinuity I used to feel.

## rotting. (signed off)

*rotting.* was written in recovery from a psychosis, while I was processing what had
happened and how I felt during it. A lot of what it explores reaches beyond the
psychosis, though. It's about identity fragmenting to the point where the self
dissolves, and the crucible of what's left: someone lost, confused, in need, alone,
pleading, desperate. I wanted to expose that part of myself.

If the way we are is a state, what's at the bottom? What's left without one? What if
every capability you have to understand, to intellectualise, to tell the internal from
the external, were gone? Are we anything without them?

The idea had been brewing for a while. During that time I'd recorded myself on my phone
playing guitar and singing. Those sessions were a way to cope, and I recorded them out of
habit, not because I meant to use them. Once I had an Octatrack and was comfortable with
it, it was only a matter of time before the idea and the recordings came together. Making
it out of past fragments, the recordings from that time and the rest of my own material,
felt inevitable.

Each track is one Octatrack project with a single pattern. I chose the samples and
mangled them into a shape I liked. Then I used scenes and the crossfader to make
movement, modulation, transitions and sections, and performed each one live, mixing by
hand from scene to scene. That's what you hear.

## hope as haunting (not settled: what is the record itself saying?)

### Current version

*hope as haunting* follows on from *the past in progress*. That record heard the past
chewing on itself, the haunting Mark Fisher and others described, and the answer was
already in it: a shell recursing on itself, a fossil, a title that says the past is still
in progress. Hauntology assumes something was once truly new. But reality, physics, the
earth, the limits of perception have always bounded what's possible, and climbing a rung
doesn't put you somewhere that can ignore the ladder or the rungs below it. Nothing was
ever finished enough to be called repeatable. The fragments we leave behind get found and
treated as new, and maybe that isn't a bug but a feature.

It's built from RF recordings I made with a SOMA Ether, mixed with solo guitar passages
that float unbound. The guitar always sits behind the noise, and it's never lost. The
last track, *wired connection*, is made the *rotting.* way instead. Its texture is
different, but it still fits with the rest.

### Cut from the current version (felt tacked on and unrelated; kept here)

Maybe hope, even hope born inside the system that taints it, can heal anyway. It stops
being "look at all this static in the way of the beauty" and becomes "finding the beauty
in the static is the point."

### Earlier version (before the "nothing was ever finished" idea)

*hope as haunting* answers *the past in progress*. That record heard the past chewing on
itself, the haunting Mark Fisher and others described, as a trap. This one wonders if
it's the point. The fragments we leave behind get found by someone else and treated as
new. Maybe that isn't a bug but a feature. And maybe hope, even hope born inside the
system that taints it, can heal anyway. It stops being "look at all this static in the
way of the beauty" and becomes "finding the beauty in the static is the point."

### First version (closest to the original answer)

*hope as haunting* feels like a response to *the past in progress*. I found a palette I
liked and wanted to communicate a widening of the sense of haunting in myself: the
feelings and patterns Mark Fisher and many others have described. What if none of this is
new? What if the fragments we leave behind, rediscovered and treated as though they were
new, aren't a bug but a feature? What if hope, even self-generated hope, can heal anyway,
however tainted it is by the system it grew up in? Then it looks less like "look at all
this static in the way of the beauty" and more like "finding the beauty in the static is
the point."
